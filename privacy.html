Privacy Policy

The short version: everything about your listening stays on your iPhone and, if you leave iCloud sync on, in your own iCloud. The app has no accounts, no advertising and no tracking. A few anonymous counts reach us so we can see which topics people care about; nothing that identifies you.

Effective 25 October 2026 (replaces the version of 14 September 2026) · applies to the Jung Helpdesk app for iOS and the Android waitlist on this website

The Jung Helpdesk app is a companion to the C.G. Jung Helpdesk podcast. It is published by Marcus Nieder, Ferdinandstr. 14, 12621 Berlin, Germany, who is the controller in the sense of the GDPR for the little data described below. For anything about this policy, write to jung-helpdesk@mail.de.

Impressum (legal notice under § 5 DDG): cgjunghelpdesk.github.io/impressum.html

What stays on your phone

The app keeps the following only in its own storage on your device. It is never uploaded anywhere by the app.

  • Where you are in each episode, which episodes you finished, and which you marked as played.
  • Episodes you hearted, the topics you picked, and the bookmarks and notes you keep.
  • Downloaded audio and transcripts for offline listening, and your settings (playback speed, intro trimming, notifications).

Deleting the app deletes all of it. We keep no copy. If iCloud sync is on (it is, unless you switch it off under Settings → Your data), the same progress is stored in your own iCloud account, in the app's small key-value store, so your other iPhones and a reinstall pick it up. That store belongs to your Apple account; we have no access to it. You can also use Save a copy: it writes a file you own, to Files, iCloud Drive, AirDrop or mail. We never see that file either.

What leaves your phone

1. Fetching the podcast itself

Like every podcast app, this one downloads the feed, the audio and the transcripts from where the podcast hosts them. Those servers see your IP address and the app's name, the same as when you stream in any player.

ServiceWhat it deliversWho runs it
RedCircleThe episode feed and the audio filesRedCircle Technologies, USA: their policy
GitHubThe transcripts, the app's topic catalog, the glossary and the transcript word-fix list, all public filesGitHub, Inc.
MeetupThe group's public event calendar, for the "next live evening" cardMeetup LLC

None of these requests carry anything about you beyond the connection itself. The app sends no account, no name and no device identifier.

2. Anonymous usage signals

To learn which topics and episodes matter to listeners, the app sends a handful of anonymous events to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service based in Germany that is built around not identifying people (their policy). Each event carries a hashed, salted identifier that cannot be turned back into your device or you. We cannot look up an individual person, and neither can TelemetryDeck.

The complete list of what is sent:

  • Topic picked: the topic's name, when you choose topics during onboarding.
  • Episode finished: the episode number.
  • Journey finished: the journey's name.
  • Search term: only a term you actually submit (Return, or a tapped suggestion), lowercased and cut at 40 characters. Text you type and abandon is never sent.
  • Transcript correction: when you report a mistake in a transcript: the episode, the timestamp, the wrong word, and the correction you optionally typed. Please don't put personal details into that field; it is meant for a word or two.
  • Tip given: that a tip or a supporter subscription went through. No amount, no identifier.

That is everything. Not your listening positions, not your likes, not your downloads, not the time of day, not your location.

3. Notifications

If you turn on new-episode notifications, iOS wakes the app now and then, the app checks the feed itself and shows a local notification. There is no push server and no token sent anywhere. You can switch it off in the app or in iOS Settings at any time.

4. Crash reports

The app uses no crash-reporting service. If you have allowed Apple to share diagnostics with developers, Apple may pass us anonymised crash logs under Apple's own terms.

5. Tips and the supporter subscription

If you tip the show or subscribe as a supporter, the purchase runs entirely through Apple's In-App Purchase. Apple handles the payment and knows who you are; we receive the proceeds and a count, never your name, address or card. Subscriptions are managed and cancelled in your Apple account settings. The app sends one anonymous signal that a tip happened, with no amount and no identifier.

6. Siri, Shortcuts, widgets and CarPlay

These are features of iOS that run on your device and talk to the app locally. Nothing about what you ask Siri, or what the widget shows, is sent to us.

7. Meetup events

The app fetches the group's public event calendar from meetup.com to show the next live evening. Meetup sees the request like any website visit; nothing about you is sent along. Adding an event to your calendar happens through the iOS calendar sheet and stays on your device.

The Android waitlist on this website

The app is coming to Android. If you leave your email address on this website to hear when it is out, this is what happens with it.

  • What is kept: your email address, and the time and IP address of your sign-up and of your confirmation, which the law asks us to be able to show.
  • What for: one email to confirm your address, and one email when the Android app is released. Nothing else: no newsletter, no marketing, no passing on.
  • Who keeps it: MailerLite (UAB "MailerLite", Vilnius, Lithuania, their policy), which stores the list and sends the two emails on our behalf under a data processing agreement.
  • Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), given by signing up and confirming the email we send.
  • How long: until you unsubscribe, and at the latest until the release email has gone out; then the list is deleted.
  • Leaving: every email has a one-click unsubscribe link, or write to jung-helpdesk@mail.de and we remove you.

What we don't do

  • No accounts, no sign-in, and the app collects no email address. The only email addresses we keep are those on the Android waitlist above.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone. The anonymous counts above are used only to shape the podcast and the app.
  • No data from children knowingly; the app is not directed at children under 13.

Legal basis and retention

Where the GDPR applies, the anonymous usage signals rest on our legitimate interest in understanding how the app and podcast are used (Art. 6(1)(f)). Because the signals cannot be tied to a person, they are not personal data in our hands once received; TelemetryDeck keeps them in aggregated form. Everything else stays on your device for as long as you keep the app.

Your choices

  • Delete the app to remove every trace of your listening from the device.
  • Turn notifications off in the app or in iOS Settings.
  • Ask us anything about this policy at jung-helpdesk@mail.de. Because we hold nothing that identifies you, we cannot retrieve or delete "your" records: there are none to find.

Changes
